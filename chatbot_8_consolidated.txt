Q1: What is robotics?
A1: Robotics is an interdisciplinary field that combines mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, computer science, and artificial intelligence to design, build, and operate robots that can perform tasks autonomously or with human assistance.

Q2: What are the main components of a robot?
A2: The main components of a robot include:

Sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR, accelerometers) for perception
Actuators (e.g., motors, servos) for movement
Controller (microcontroller or computer) for processing
Power source (batteries or external supply)
End-effectors (grippers, tools) for performing tasks
Q3: What are the different types of robots?
A3: Robots can be categorized into:

Industrial robots (used in manufacturing, welding, assembly)
Autonomous robots (self-driving cars, drones)
Humanoid robots (designed to resemble humans, e.g., ASIMO)
Medical robots (surgical robots, prosthetics)
Service robots (vacuum cleaners, delivery bots)
Q4: What is the role of artificial intelligence in robotics?
A4: AI in robotics enables robots to perceive, learn, and make decisions based on data. It includes:

Machine learning for pattern recognition
Computer vision for object detection
Natural language processing for communication
Path planning for navigation
Q5: What is the difference between autonomous and semi-autonomous robots?
A5: Autonomous robots operate without human intervention, using sensors and AI for decision-making (e.g., self-driving cars). Semi-autonomous robots require some level of human control or supervision (e.g., drones with manual override).

Q6: What are robotic sensors and their functions?
A6: Robotic sensors help robots perceive their environment. Examples include:

Ultrasonic sensors – Detect obstacles using sound waves
Infrared sensors – Measure heat and distance
Vision sensors (cameras) – Capture images for processing
Gyroscopes & accelerometers – Detect motion and orientation
Q7: What is the kinematics of a robot?
A7: Robot kinematics deals with the movement of robotic arms and mechanisms. It includes:

Forward kinematics – Determining the end position from joint angles
Inverse kinematics – Calculating joint angles to reach a desired position
Differential kinematics – Studying small changes in motion
Q8: What are some real-world applications of robotics?
A8: Robotics is used in:

Manufacturing – Assembly lines, welding robots
Healthcare – Robotic surgery, rehabilitation devices
Agriculture – Automated harvesting, drones for monitoring
Military & defense – Bomb disposal robots, surveillance drones
Space exploration – Mars rovers, robotic arms on spacecraft
Q9: What are the challenges in robotics development?
A9: Some key challenges include:

Complex decision-making in unpredictable environments
High cost of development and maintenance
Energy efficiency and battery limitations
Human-robot interaction and safety concerns
Ethical and legal considerations in AI-powered robots
Q10: What is swarm robotics?
A10: Swarm robotics is a field that studies how multiple robots can work together as a group, inspired by natural swarms like ants or bees. It is used in tasks such as environmental monitoring, search and rescue, and cooperative transport.
